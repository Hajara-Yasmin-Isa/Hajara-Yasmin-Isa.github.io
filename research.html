Code Generation to Concept Learning: Student Use of LLMs in a Web Programming Course

Hajara-Yasmin Isa; Matthew Weston; Muhammad Rizky Wellyanto; Ishita Karna; Jerry O. Talton; Ranjitha Kumar

ACM CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems (CHI ‘26), Barcelona, Spain

PDF DOI
While prior work has examined LLM usage through surveys and controlled studies in introductory courses, a gap exists in studying organic usage patterns in advanced coursework where assignments mirror real-world software challenges. We analyzed 448 LLM chat logs from 147 students enrolled in a web programming course during Fall 2023 and Fall 2024. Through open coding, we identified 14 distinct prompt-response pairs that cluster into three categories: generate code, debug code, and explain programming concepts. We examined how student prompt-response pairs were distributed across course assignments and how different LLM interaction behaviors correlate with individual performance outcomes.

No Wires, Just Wear: Monitoring Breathing With Smart Fabrics

Hajara-Yasmin Isa; Robin Kravets

ACM International Conference on Mobile Systems, Applications, and Services (MobiSys ‘25)

PDF DOI
Wearable sensing technologies offer promising solutions for non-intrusive monitoring in healthcare, fitness, and assistive applications. This paper presents a non-intrusive method for monitoring respiration using an off-the-shelf stretchable conductive fabric that serves as the sensor itself—eliminating the need for embedded components. Real-time breathing patterns are captured by measuring the fabric’s inherent resistance changes during chest expansion and contraction. The system is simple and does not require complex circuit fabrication, making it especially well-suited for continuous monitoring in contexts where comfort and minimal disruption are essential.
FIE ‘24

GA2Graph: Visualizing Online Collaboration Patterns in Computer Science Education

Abdussalam Alawini, Hajara-Yasmin Isa, Jiabao Xu, Yutong Zhang, and Zhijun Zhao

IEEE Frontiers in Education Conference (FIE ‘24)

PDF DOI
This innovative practice full paper describes an approach to visualizing and analyzing collaborative learning. Collaborative learning is vital for enriching computer science education and developing key skills. However, educators often lack tools for effectively tracking and analyzing student engagement in group work. Our study introduces GA2Graph, a data-driven system that uses Neo4j to visualize and analyze online collaborative patterns in classroom activities. It graphically represents students and their interactions, helping educators understand group dynamics. We tested this system in a large-enrollment database course, revealing that despite emphasis on assigned roles like those in POGIL, students frequently disregarded them and adopted more individualistic or varied collaborative strategies.
